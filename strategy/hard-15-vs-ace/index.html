Hard 15 vs dealer ace: hit, stand, or surrender?

It depends on the soft 17 rule. If the dealer hits soft 17, surrender (hit if surrender is not offered). If the dealer stands on soft 17, hit.

  • Dealer hits soft 17SurrenderIf surrender is not offered: hit
  • Dealer stands on soft 17Hit
  • Hit: take one more card.
  • Surrender: give up the hand at once for a smaller, fixed loss. Only as your first decision, and only where the table offers it.

Why

Dealer hits soft 17. Some hands are bad enough that surrendering half the hand beats playing them out. This is one of the few spots where cutting losses is the mathematically best play.

Dealer stands on soft 17. A dealer ace rarely busts, so standing on a stiff total loses more often than not. Taking a card gives you a better shot.

Hard 15 means no ace is being counted as 11. Two-card ways to make it: 10 + 5, 9 + 6, 8 + 7 (a 10 can be a 10, jack, queen, or king).

The numbers

  • A dealer ace busts about 20% of the time when the dealer hits soft 17 and 17% when the dealer stands on soft 17, counted after the dealer has checked for blackjack.
  • Taking one card on a hard 15 busts about 54% of the time.

Rules assumed

  • 4, 6, or 8 decks (the chart is the same for all three)
  • The dealer checks for blackjack when showing a 10 or an ace, before you act
  • Double down is allowed on any first two cards
  • Double after split is allowed
  • Late surrender is offered, as your first decision only
  • Split up to four hands; split aces get one card each and cannot be resplit

One-deck and two-deck games, tables without surrender, and dealers who do not check for blackjack change some plays. Check your table's rules before relying on any chart.

Common questions: Hard 15 vs dealer ace

What is the correct play for hard 15 against a dealer ace?

It depends on the soft 17 rule. If the dealer hits soft 17, surrender (hit if surrender is not offered). If the dealer stands on soft 17, hit. Both assume 4 to 8 decks.

Does the soft 17 rule change hard 15 against a dealer ace?

Yes. If the dealer hits soft 17, surrender (hit if surrender is not offered). If the dealer stands on soft 17, hit. Only a handful of hands on the chart change with that rule, and this is one of them.

What if surrender is not offered?

If the dealer hits soft 17 and surrender is not offered, hit. If the dealer stands on soft 17, hit whether or not you can surrender. Late surrender is only offered as your first decision, after the dealer has checked for blackjack, and not every table offers it.

Why surrender with hard 15 against a dealer ace when the dealer hits soft 17?

When the dealer hits soft 17: Some hands are bad enough that surrendering half the hand beats playing them out. This is one of the few spots where cutting losses is the mathematically best play. When the dealer stands on soft 17: A dealer ace rarely busts, so standing on a stiff total loses more often than not. Taking a card gives you a better shot.

Does the play change if hard 15 has more than two cards?

Yes. Double down and surrender are only available on your first two cards, so with three or more cards, hit.

Does this assume the dealer has already checked for blackjack?

Yes. In US-style games the dealer checks for blackjack when showing an ace before you act, so this play applies once the dealer is known not to have one. In games where the dealer does not check, some plays change.

Should I take insurance when the dealer shows an ace?

No. Basic strategy always declines insurance: insurance is a bad bet.

Does basic strategy guarantee a win?

No. It picks the play with the best average result, but any single hand can go either way, and the house keeps a small edge. This site and the Blackjack Trainer app are for practice only.
