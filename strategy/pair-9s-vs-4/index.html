Pair of 9s vs dealer 4: hit, stand, or split?

Split. Pair of 9s against a dealer 4 is played the same way whether the dealer hits or stands on soft 17.

  • Dealer hits soft 17Split
  • Dealer stands on soft 17Split
  • Split: separate a pair into two hands and play each one on its own.

Why

Against most dealer cards, two hands starting on 9 do better than one 18. The exceptions are a dealer 7, 10, or ace, where 18 already beats them enough as is.

A pair is two cards of the same value, and any two 10-value cards count as a pair of 10s. Played as one hand, this pair is a hard 18: see Hard 18 vs dealer 4.

The numbers

  • A dealer 4 busts about 40% of the time.
  • Kept as one hand, this pair is a hard 18, and one hit busts it about 77% of the time.

Rules assumed

  • 4, 6, or 8 decks (the chart is the same for all three)
  • The dealer checks for blackjack when showing a 10 or an ace, before you act
  • Double down is allowed on any first two cards
  • Double after split is allowed
  • Late surrender is offered, as your first decision only
  • Split up to four hands; split aces get one card each and cannot be resplit

One-deck and two-deck games, tables without surrender, and dealers who do not check for blackjack change some plays. Check your table's rules before relying on any chart.

Common questions: Pair of 9s vs dealer 4

What is the correct play for a pair of 9s against a dealer 4?

Split. This is basic strategy for 4 to 8 decks, and it is the same whether the dealer hits or stands on soft 17.

Does the soft 17 rule change a pair of 9s against a dealer 4?

No. The play is the same whether the dealer hits or stands on soft 17. Only a handful of hands on the chart change with that rule, and this is not one of them.

Should I split 9s against a dealer 4?

Yes. Against most dealer cards, two hands starting on 9 do better than one 18. The exceptions are a dealer 7, 10, or ace, where 18 already beats them enough as is.

Does basic strategy guarantee a win?

No. It picks the play with the best average result, but any single hand can go either way, and the house keeps a small edge. This site and the Blackjack Trainer app are for practice only.
