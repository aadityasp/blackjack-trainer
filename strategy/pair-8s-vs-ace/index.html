Pair of 8s vs dealer ace: hit, stand, split, or surrender?

It depends on the soft 17 rule. If the dealer hits soft 17, surrender (split if surrender is not offered). If the dealer stands on soft 17, split.

  • Dealer hits soft 17SurrenderIf surrender is not offered: split
  • Dealer stands on soft 17Split
  • Split: separate a pair into two hands and play each one on its own.
  • Surrender: give up the hand at once for a smaller, fixed loss. Only as your first decision, and only where the table offers it.

Why

Dealer hits soft 17. Some hands are bad enough that surrendering half the hand beats playing them out. This is one of the few spots where cutting losses is the mathematically best play.

Dealer stands on soft 17. A hard 16 loses more than it wins no matter what the dealer shows. Splitting turns one bad hand into two hands starting from a playable 8.

A pair is two cards of the same value, and any two 10-value cards count as a pair of 10s. Played as one hand, this pair is a hard 16: see Hard 16 vs dealer ace.

The numbers

  • A dealer ace busts about 20% of the time when the dealer hits soft 17 and 17% when the dealer stands on soft 17, counted after the dealer has checked for blackjack.
  • Kept as one hand, this pair is a hard 16, and one hit busts it about 62% of the time.

Rules assumed

  • 4, 6, or 8 decks (the chart is the same for all three)
  • The dealer checks for blackjack when showing a 10 or an ace, before you act
  • Double down is allowed on any first two cards
  • Double after split is allowed
  • Late surrender is offered, as your first decision only
  • Split up to four hands; split aces get one card each and cannot be resplit

One-deck and two-deck games, tables without surrender, and dealers who do not check for blackjack change some plays. Check your table's rules before relying on any chart.

Common questions: Pair of 8s vs dealer ace

What is the correct play for a pair of 8s against a dealer ace?

It depends on the soft 17 rule. If the dealer hits soft 17, surrender (split if surrender is not offered). If the dealer stands on soft 17, split. Both assume 4 to 8 decks.

Does the soft 17 rule change a pair of 8s against a dealer ace?

Yes. If the dealer hits soft 17, surrender (split if surrender is not offered). If the dealer stands on soft 17, split. Only a handful of hands on the chart change with that rule, and this is one of them.

Should I split 8s against a dealer ace?

It depends on the soft 17 rule. If the dealer hits soft 17, surrender (split if surrender is not offered). If the dealer stands on soft 17, split. When the dealer hits soft 17: Some hands are bad enough that surrendering half the hand beats playing them out. This is one of the few spots where cutting losses is the mathematically best play. When the dealer stands on soft 17: A hard 16 loses more than it wins no matter what the dealer shows. Splitting turns one bad hand into two hands starting from a playable 8.

What if surrender is not offered?

If the dealer hits soft 17 and surrender is not offered, split. If the dealer stands on soft 17, split whether or not you can surrender. Late surrender is only offered as your first decision, after the dealer has checked for blackjack, and not every table offers it.

Does this assume the dealer has already checked for blackjack?

Yes. In US-style games the dealer checks for blackjack when showing an ace before you act, so this play applies once the dealer is known not to have one. In games where the dealer does not check, some plays change.

Should I take insurance when the dealer shows an ace?

No. Basic strategy always declines insurance: insurance is a bad bet.

Does basic strategy guarantee a win?

No. It picks the play with the best average result, but any single hand can go either way, and the house keeps a small edge. This site and the Blackjack Trainer app are for practice only.
